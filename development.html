03 / PERSONAL DEVELOPMENT

Small habits. Lasting growth.

Reflections on George S. Clason’s The Richest Man in Babylon.

CHAPTER THREE · PAGES 22–42

Seven Cures for a Lean Purse

In this chapter, Arkad, Babylon’s richest man, shares seven ways people can improve their financial situation. The lesson I take from this is that financial habits matter even when someone is not earning much. Consistency, rather than waiting for the perfect opportunity, is a practical starting point.

  1. Start thy purse to fattening.

    Save a portion of the money I receive before spending on wants. As a student, even a small regular amount can build the habit.

  2. Control thy expenditures.

    Separate needs from wants and plan a realistic budget. This helps me make room for school costs without spending impulsively.

  3. Make thy gold multiply.

    Learn how money can grow responsibly over time. Before investing, I would research carefully and understand the risks.

  4. Guard thy treasures from loss.

    Protect savings by avoiding decisions I do not understand and by seeking reliable advice instead of acting on pressure.

  5. Make of thy dwelling a profitable investment.

    Think carefully about long-term living costs and how a stable, suitable home can support financial wellbeing.

  6. Insure a future income.

    Plan ahead for future responsibilities instead of assuming today’s income or support will always be available.

  7. Increase thy ability to earn.

    Keep learning and strengthening skills. My studies, design practice, and professional experience can help me create future opportunities.

CHAPTER FIVE · PAGES 59–73

The Five Laws of Gold

Through Nomasir, Arkad’s son, Clason shows that receiving money is not enough: financial understanding is needed to protect and grow it. The chapter reminds me to make decisions carefully and to be wary of promises that sound too good to be true.

  1. Gold comes to those who consistently save.

    Saving regularly gives me a foundation for emergencies and future goals.

  2. Gold multiplies when put to profitable use.

    Money can work toward future goals when used thoughtfully, with realistic expectations and a clear understanding of risk.

  3. Gold remains with the cautious owner who seeks wise advice.

    I should learn from people with relevant knowledge and check advice before acting on it.

  4. Gold is lost when invested in unfamiliar ventures.

    I should not invest in something just because it is popular or someone pressures me to join.

  5. Gold escapes those who pursue unrealistic profits or trust dishonest schemes.

    Guaranteed wealth and fast-profit promises deserve skepticism. Patience and verification are safer than greed.

MY BIGGEST TAKEAWAY

Financial success begins with how I use what I already have.

Before reading these chapters, it can be tempting to think that becoming wealthy depends mainly on earning a high salary or having access to many opportunities. Clason’s lessons encourage me to start with the choices available to me now: save consistently, plan spending, build my skills, and make informed decisions. The habits I develop as a student can shape how I manage income and responsibilities as an adult. I also learned that progress requires patience; lasting results usually come from repeated good decisions rather than one dramatic achievement.
