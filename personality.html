The person behind the portfolio.

My interests, values, and emotions all play a part in how I learn and grow.

TRAIT 01

Creativity

Creativity helps me explore ideas, approach assignments from different angles, and express what I understand visually. Drawing and macramé give me space to experiment, practise patience, and develop solutions instead of settling for the first idea.

Academic impact: helps with design thinking, visual communication, and original project ideas.

TRAIT 02

Determination

Determination encourages me to keep going when a task becomes difficult. It reminds me that progress takes practice, and that mistakes can be useful feedback rather than a reason to give up.

Academic impact: supports persistence with challenging topics, deadlines, and improving work through revision.

TRAIT 03

Sensitivity

Sensitivity helps me notice emotions and consider other people’s perspectives. It can make collaboration more thoughtful and help me respond with empathy when classmates are experiencing challenges.

Academic impact: encourages respectful teamwork, reflection, and awareness of how others learn.

AI & STUDENTSHIP

A tool, not a crutch

ChatGPT, Claude, and Copilot can explain complex concepts, suggest ideas, and give feedback. To me, the statement “LLMs must be used as a tool, not a crutch” means that AI should support my learning rather than replace my thinking, effort, or responsibility. The purpose of education is not simply to submit completed assignments; it is to become more knowledgeable, independent, and able to judge information critically.

I can use an LLM when I am stuck on a difficult concept, need practice questions, want feedback on the clarity of a paragraph, or need help planning a study schedule. I should use these tools in a way that supports learning: ask for explanations, attempt the task myself, verify important claims with credible sources, and decide which suggestions are useful. AI can make mistakes or present biased or incomplete information, so copying an answer without checking it can weaken my understanding and academic integrity.

For example, when learning HTML and CSS, I can ask AI to explain why a layout behaves a certain way, then test and write the code myself. I should follow my lecturer’s rules, disclose AI assistance when required, and never submit generated work as proof of skills I have not developed. Used responsibly, AI can be a study partner; used as a crutch, it can prevent me from building the very skills I am meant to learn.
